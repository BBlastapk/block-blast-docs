# Recovery Guide

Every Block Blast player eventually reaches a difficult board position. Recovery is the process of restoring flexibility instead of allowing the board to become completely blocked.

Strong recovery skills often separate experienced players from beginners.

---

## Recognize Early Warning Signs

Recovery becomes much easier when problems are identified early.

Common warning signs include:

- Limited placement options
- Crowded central area
- Several isolated empty spaces
- Large pieces becoming difficult to place
- Repeated low-value moves

Taking action early usually produces better results than waiting until only one move remains.

---

## Reduce Board Pressure

When the board becomes crowded, the primary goal changes from scoring to creating usable space.

Helpful approaches include:

- Clear rows or columns whenever practical.
- Connect separated open areas.
- Remove awkward gaps.
- Avoid adding unnecessary obstacles.

Think about improving the next five moves rather than the next single move.

---

## Protect Flexible Space

Large connected areas increase the number of possible placements.

During recovery:

- Avoid dividing the board into smaller sections.
- Preserve open lanes.
- Keep several placement choices available.
- Delay risky placements when safer alternatives exist.

---

## Stay Patient

Recovery usually happens over several moves.

Avoid making desperate placements simply because they produce immediate points.

Instead:

- Evaluate multiple options.
- Choose the move that improves future flexibility.
- Continue reducing board pressure step by step.

Small improvements often lead to complete recovery.

---

## Learn From Difficult Boards

After finishing a challenging game, review the board and identify what caused the problem.

Questions worth asking include:

- Which move created the first difficult position?
- Could open space have been preserved longer?
- Were points prioritized over flexibility?
- Did isolated gaps begin forming earlier than expected?

Regular review helps improve future decision making.
